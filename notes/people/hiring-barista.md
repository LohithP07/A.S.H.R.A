# Hiring a Barista

**Role:** Barista, part time (24-30 hours), weekend availability required
**Pay:** $17.50-$19.50/hour plus pooled tips (average $4.10/hour)
**Owner:** Maya Chen

## Scorecard
- **Hospitality:** makes people feel welcome without performing. Handles a rushed line with grace.
- **Consistency:** follows a recipe and asks when unsure. Does not "fix" things silently.
- **Curiosity:** wants to learn coffee, not just use it as a paycheck.
- **Reliability:** arrives early, communicates schedule changes, can work Saturdays.
- **Team:** washes dishes without being asked and gives credit generously.

## Process
1. **Application:** short form, no cover letter. Two questions: a time you recovered a bad guest experience; what you drink and why.
2. **Phone screen (20 min):** availability, pay expectations, why this shop.
3. **Working interview (2 hours, paid):** shadow on the register, wash dishes, make one drip coffee. We are watching warmth, not technique.
4. **Reference check:** two references, one from a manager.
5. **Offer:** within 48 hours. Start date and training schedule in writing.

## Interview questions
- Tell me about a shift that went sideways. What did you do first?
- A guest says their latte is cold and the line is out the door. Walk me through the next two minutes.
- What is something you learned from a job that you would bring here?
- What do you need from a manager to do your best work?

## Red flags
- Speaks poorly about every previous employer.
- Wants full-time hours but cannot work weekends.
- Will not taste coffee or says they dislike all coffee. (Not fatal, but a signal.)
- Pushes for a salary before understanding the role.

## Onboarding
First week is 60% shadowing, 40% guided practice. No solo bar shifts before sign-off on espresso, register, and allergens. See [[Training Manual]] and [[Opening Checklist]].
